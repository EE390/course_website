Overview

The final project is your chance to apply what you have learned in the course. You will design, implement and demonstrate a functional embedded system that integrates hardware and software to solve a practical problem or perform a specific task. The work is split into four milestones, so you get feedback and can improve your design before the final evaluation.

For inspiration, browse the Cornell ECE 4760 final projects ↗. Your project is not expected to reach the same level of complexity; choose a scope that is feasible for this course.

Requirements

  1. Implement a physical prototype using a microcontroller and relevant electronic components.
  2. Develop the project software in C or assembly. You may choose either language, but you must be able to explain your code.
  3. Integrate the hardware and software into a working system.
  4. Test the system and document the verification results.
  5. Present a working prototype in the final live demonstration.

Project-specific requirements are finalized when your proposal is approved.

Milestone 1: Project proposal

Each team submits a proposal and must have it approved before starting full implementation or making significant purchases. Include:

  • Project title and team members (names and student IDs)
  • Problem statement, objectives and expected functions
  • Preliminary system block diagram and implementation approach
  • Component list, estimated budget and proposed suppliers

Milestone 2: Check-off 1

By this check-off your team must have ordered or purchased the components the project needs. Submit a short report (about one page) that:

  • Confirms all project components are ordered or purchased
  • Shows completed work and current functionality
  • Explains problems encountered and the troubleshooting you did
  • Keeps a brief log of progress and individual contributions

Milestone 3: Check-off 2

Submit a short report (about one page) that:

  • Shows completed work and current functionality
  • Explains problems encountered and the troubleshooting you did
  • Identifies remaining tasks and next-week objectives
  • Keeps a brief log of progress and individual contributions

Milestone 4: Final report and live demonstration

Live demonstration.

  • Present a fully integrated prototype and demonstrate its main functions.
  • Explain the system architecture, circuit connections and program operation.
  • Show relevant testing results and discuss limitations.
  • Answer technical questions and explain individual contributions.

Final report. Include an introduction, objectives, system block diagram, implementation, tests and results, limitations, conclusion and references. Attach the complete source code (C or assembly) and the relevant design files.

Budget & reimbursement

Subject to departmental confirmation, reimbursement of up to 200 SAR per team may be available for eligible project expenses after a successful working final demonstration. Keep your original invoices and receipts.

Eligibility, documentation and approval procedures will be announced if the policy is confirmed. Reimbursement is not guaranteed.

PCB design bonus Optional

For extra credit, turn part of your prototype into a custom PCB.

  • Deliverables: schematic, board layout and exported manufacturing files (e.g. Gerber), by the end of Week 13/14.
  • What we look for: a correct schematic, sensible component placement and routing, passing design-rule checks, and a short note on your design decisions.
  • Fabrication: boards are made by the Dream Realization Lab (DRL). Submit your fabrication request through the DRL services page ↗.
  • Software: you may use PCB design software such as KiCad for the schematic, layout and manufacturing files.

AI use policy

You may use AI tools in this project, as long as you are transparent about it and you understand everything you submit. You are responsible for all code and hardware in your project, and the staff may ask you to explain any part of it.
